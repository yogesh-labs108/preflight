# Why listeners quit a serialized audio show

Read the source novel as a listener will hear it. A chapter that works on the page can lose a listener. The page lets a reader skim, glance back at a name, and look at a stat table; audio allows none of that.

Each cause names what to look for in the text and the usual fix. Quote the line that proves the cause. If no line proves it, the cause does not apply.

## Chapter break is not an episode break
The draft split ends an episode where a chapter ends at rest, even though a stronger line sits inside the next chapter.
Look for: a draft episode whose last line is calm, when a chapter nearby has a line that opens a question.
Fix: move the episode boundary to that line, even mid-chapter.

## Reader-only devices
Things that only work on a page: status screens, stat tables, bracketed system messages, footnotes, author notes.
Look for: `[Strength: 12]`, lists of stats, "Author's note".
Fix: turn the one number that matters into a spoken line, and cut the rest.

## Late hook
The first episode spends its opening on world, backstory, or daily routine before anything goes wrong.
Look for: the inciting event arriving after the first third of episode 1.
Fix: open inside the event. Move setup after the first turn, or drop it.

## Exposition dump
Rules of the world read out as a list: realms, ranks, sect structure, system menus. A reader can skim a list. A listener cannot.
Look for: three or more named ranks, levels, or rules in one passage.
Fix: introduce one rule at the moment the hero needs it. Cut the rest.

## Payoff drought
Episode 1 makes a promise (the humiliated hero will rise, the secret will come out) and the next episodes keep it waiting, or tell the payoff in one line instead of playing it as a scene.
Look for: the promise from episode 1 not delivered as a scene by episode 5. Payoffs written as summary ("he passed easily").
Fix: deliver a small payoff every one to two episodes, played out in full.

## Closed ending
The episode resolves its own tension and ends at rest.
Look for: final lines about sleeping, going home, feeling better, things being fine.
Fix: end on an open question the next episode must answer. Often it is enough to move the first beat of the next episode to the end of this one.

## Name flood
Too many named characters arrive at once. In audio there is no page to glance back at.
Look for: four or more new names in one episode, or two characters with similar names.
Fix: at most two new names per episode. Give the rest roles, not names ("the steward").

## Hero missing
An early episode follows someone other than the hero: a scheming elder, a rival's family.
Look for: an episode among the first five where the hero barely appears.
Fix: cut it, or shrink it to a short scene that ends an episode as a cliffhanger.

## Recap bloat
Episodes retell what the listener just heard. Common when webnovel chapters are adapted one to one.
Look for: an episode opening that summarises earlier events for more than a few lines.
Fix: one line of recap at most, or none.

## Audio confusion
Scene or speaker changes the listener cannot hear.
Look for: a scene cut with no sound or line that marks it, or dialogue with no speaker cue.
Fix: mark each scene change with a line about place or time, and name the speaker in the first line after a switch.
